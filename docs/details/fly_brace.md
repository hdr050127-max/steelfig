# Fly brace: rafter bottom flange to purlin

**Parts**: angle each side of the rafter; one bolt through the purlin web and the angle leg; a cleat plate welded
to the rafter flange tip with one bolt.
**Rules** (`assembly.fly_braces_on`):
- angle 50×50×5 EA + M12 for rafters up to 360 deep, 75×50×6 UA + M16 above (ASSUMPTION)
- long leg flat on the flange-free face of the purlin web: upslope face of a Z (its bottom flange is downslope),
  back of a C; the leg's short leg points away from the web
- web bolt at mid-depth of the purlin web, ≥ 1.5 d_f from each flange and from the angle end; angle end stops
  15 mm under the top flange (ASSUMPTION for the exact numbers)
- brace at ~45°: outward reach = heel offset + rafter depth + 85 mm + bolt height; checked within 35–55°
- lower end 85 mm below the flange underside so the 75 mm leg clears the flange; cleat 120×70×8 in the brace
  plane, hanging from the flange tip, one bolt 40 mm from the angle end
**Sources**: purlin manufacturers' manuals (Stramit, Lysaght) for the purlin orientation and the fly-brace
principle; course photo (tutor p10: braces from the bottom chord to purlins, both sides); AS 4100 Cl 9.6 for the
bolt distances; draftsperson.net "Typical Fly Brace Detail" (free CAD block) for the overall arrangement.
**Update 2026-11-04 (Stramit guide, `sources/stramit-guide.md`)**: the fly brace bolts to the purlin web through
the lower lap / cleat hole, B = 55 mm above the purlin bottom for the 150-250 series (not mid-web); welding to
purlins is not recommended, so the purlin end is always bolted.
